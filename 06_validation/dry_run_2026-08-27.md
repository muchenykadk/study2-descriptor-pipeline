## Diagnosis

**Three fragment-level rules fire on every fragment.** `leaning_support`, `pedestal_support`
and `handling_class` return all twelve. `handling_class` is not wrong: its threshold is 800 mm
longest dimension and the shortest fragment in the corpus is 737 mm across its smallest
bounding axis but 957 mm at its longest, so every piece sits on one side of the boundary. The
rule is sound and the corpus does not exercise it. `leaning_support` and `pedestal_support`
list every face of every fragment and carry the caveat "orientation unverified", which is the
uncomputed resting pose: without it the rule cannot test the angle it exists to test, so it
admits everything.

**`connection_strategy` performs worse than returning every face.** It reaches recall 0.88
against the null's 1.00 at identical precision, and it returns 6 of 8 faces on FS-007 and 6 of
8 on FS-012, both of which took no timber connection. The cause is known: `area_m2_est` is the
convex hull of the plane inliers, not a contiguous bearing surface, so the area test admits
faces that have no continuous area to bolt to.

**The show-face decision is unreachable.** FS-008 and FS-010 carry no feature on any face, so
no query can return them. This is the face-label gap: features are classified on regions, most
regions are non-planar, and only planar faces reach the design rules.

## Caveats that must be resolved before any of this is reportable

1. Table B was not frozen first. Rule-to-row mapping was chosen with the data visible.
2. Evidence sources are empty, so the ground truth is unverified.
3. D7 is excluded. `FS-001[6]` is out of range on a 5-face fragment, and its brackets repeat
   D3's patterns exactly (`[1,3,6]`, `[5,7]`), which indicates copied text rather than
   observation.
4. D5 and D8 are empty.
5. D3 was scored as 8 independent faces. Under the comma convention it is 4 surfaces, giving
   3 of 4 rather than 7 of 8. The convention was not in the file when the row was filled, so
   the intended reading is unconfirmed.
6. FS-001 is in the pool. It has geometry but no vision labels, which depresses D6b, and at
   2,562 kg it is the heaviest fragment in the corpus while not appearing in any documented
   decision.

## Open question on D2

Documented stable bases are FS-003, FS-006 and FS-004, ranking 2nd, 4th and 7th by mass of
twelve. FS-001 ranks 1st at 2,562 kg and appears in no decision. Either it was not part of the
built prototype, in which case it should leave the pool, or mass alone did not drive the
choice, in which case D2's implicit criterion needs rewriting.
